# Prebiotic Synthesis of L-Proline: Routes, Evidence, Thermodynamics, Chirality, and Open Problems (literature to October 2026)

Only one route to proline has been worked out step by step from simple feedstocks under prebiotic conditions: the UV/H2S cyanosulfidic chemistry of Patel et al. (2015, *Nature Chemistry*). It goes HCN + acetylene → acrylonitrile → cyclic thioamide → proline α-aminonitrile.\[1\] Every other route either detects proline in mixtures (spark discharge, ice irradiation, meteorites, asteroid samples) or is a biochemistry-inspired idea with no proline step demonstrated (reductive amination over metals, glutamate → P5C, ornithine → proline). None of the abiotic routes makes L-proline selectively. All give racemic proline or racemic precursors. Getting from there to L-proline needs a separate symmetry-breaking and amplification step. Proline is unusually well studied for that step (eutectic and cosolvate amplification), but chloroform was needed to reach the highest values.

## TL;DR
- **One stepwise route, from one framework.** The only stepwise, intermediate-resolved prebiotic synthesis of the proline skeleton is cyanosulfidic. Cu(I) couples HCN and acetylene to acrylonitrile; NH3 adds; UV/H2S reduction and cyclization give γ-butyrothiolactam; further reduction plus HCN gives pyrrolidine-2-carbonitrile (32% over the final two steps). Even this route stops at the aminonitrile, with no reported hydrolysis yield to proline. Its geochemical assumptions (sulfide, Cu, acetylene, staged reagent delivery) are actively contested.
- **Everything else is detection or analogy.** Proline has been found in spark-discharge products (Ring et al. 1972), in hydrolyzed UV-irradiated interstellar ice residues (Muñoz Caro et al. 2002), in Murchison, and in Bennu samples (Glavin et al. 2025). None of these establishes a mechanism. Metal- and mineral-catalyzed reductive amination works for keto acids → Ala/Glu/Asp, and so on, but no one has shown a nonenzymatic, prebiotic reduction of glutamate (or GSA/P5C) or of ornithine to proline. That gap is the central bottleneck for metabolism-first frameworks.
- **Chirality is unsolved for proline specifically.** Asteroid samples (Ryugu, Bennu) show racemic amino acids. Meteoritic L-excesses in α-H protein amino acids remain contested as contamination or co-elution. Proline has the strongest physical-amplification data of any amino acid: a 1% ee solution can be driven toward the eutectic, up to ~99% ee in the CHCl3 cosolvate system (Klussmann et al. 2006). The aqueous-relevant eutectics are lower, and the seed ee must come from elsewhere.

### 2. The best-characterized route: cyanosulfidic chemistry (Route A)

**Framework.** Sutherland and co-workers (MRC LMB) propose "reductive homologation of hydrogen cyanide and some of its derivatives." In the abstract's words, the key steps "are driven by ultraviolet light, use hydrogen sulfide as the reductant and can be accelerated by Cu(I)–Cu(II) photoredox cycling" (Patel, Percivalle, Ritson, Duffy & Sutherland 2015, *Nature Chemistry* 7:301–307).\[2\] All C and N atoms in the network come from HCN.\[3\] The proline and arginine branches start from acrylonitrile.\[1\]\[4\]

**Steps for network design.** Compound numbering follows Patel et al. 2015; one step is inferred, as noted.
1. HCN + HC≡CH → acrylonitrile (CH2=CHCN). Catalyst: Cu(I). The paper's Figure 1 legend says: "Copper(I) catalysed cross-coupling of hydrogen cyanide and acetylene gives acrylonitrile, reductive homologation of which gives precursors of Pro and Arg."\[4\] Acetylene is proposed to come from hydrolysis of impact-derived metal carbides.
2. Acrylonitrile + NH3 → β-aminopropionitrile (aza-Michael addition, no catalyst). The authors note it is "a potential precursor of proline and lysine if the amino group … was left free, and arginine if the amino group … could somehow be guanidinylated."\[1\]
3. Nitrile → aldehyde by UV/H2S photoreduction, then HCN addition → cyanohydrin.
4. Cyanohydrin + H2S → α-hydroxythioamides. Unusually, the cyclic α-hydroxythioamide forms alongside the open-chain one.\[4\]
5. UV/H2S photodeoxygenation and cyclization → γ-butyrothiolactam. The paper reports that the thiolactam is "the only deoxygenated thioamide" from this mixture.\[4\]
6. γ-Butyrothiolactam → (further photoreduction; the cyclic imine 1-pyrroline is the presumed intermediate, inferred) + HCN → pyrrolidine-2-carbonitrile, the proline α-aminonitrile.\[1\] **Reported yield: 32% over 2 steps.**\[4\]
7. Aminonitrile → prolinamide → proline by hydrolysis. **No yield reported in the main paper**, and the authors describe the aminonitrile only as the "precursor of proline."

**Cross-talk with arginine.** If cyanamide is present when acrylonitrile is made, β-aminopropionaldehyde is guanidinylated efficiently. The paper says the arginine aminonitrile "would have been produced alongside" the proline aminonitrile.\[1\]\[4\] This branch point matters for network design: cyanamide diverts flux away from proline.\[4\]

**A proline-specific advantage.** Strecker chemistry is equilibrium-controlled before hydrolysis.\[5\] Ritson (2021, *Science Advances*, "A cyanosulfidic origin of the Krebs cycle") notes that at low NH3 or low pH, amino nitriles "would be partially, or wholly, converted to cyanohydrins [with the exception of proline amino nitrile]."\[5\] The cyclic secondary-amine aminonitrile is retained under conditions where acyclic ones revert. This makes proline's Strecker precursor unusually robust.

**Environment and conditions.** The scenario is a post-impact surface: ferrocyanide salts are evaporated and thermally metamorphosed into feedstocks, then dissolved in UV-irradiated streams or pools with sulfide.\[6\] Laboratory runs used 254 nm irradiation.\[7\]

**Published critiques.**
- *Sulfide supply.* Ranjan, Todd, Sutherland & Sasselov (2018, *Astrobiology*; arXiv:1801.07725) note that the chemistry "requires sulfidic anions to proceed, as both a photoreductant and as a feedstock," and that impact sourcing "imposes an additional, local requirement."\[7\] They modeled whether volcanic SO2/H2S could supply enough sulfidic anions to surface waters, which constrains the conditions under which this works.
- *Copper and reagent logistics.* Sulfide is poorly soluble except under alkaline conditions, and copper has low crustal abundance. Critics also note that "many of the reactions require the periodic delivery of new reagents."\[8\] Sutherland's group answers with a flow-chemistry scenario.\[9\]
- *Cyanide persistence.* Keefe & Miller (1996, *OLEB*, "Was ferrocyanide a prebiotic reagent?") questioned ferrocyanide stockpiling.\[2\] More recent geochemical modeling by Todd, Wogan & Catling (2024, *ACS Earth and Space Chemistry* 8:221–229) finds that ferrocyanide "may be able to form and reach significant concentrations for prebiotic chemistry on the time scale of years under favorable conditions," with pH 9 allowing maximum concentrations. Murali et al. (2026, arXiv:2608.00025) report new hydrolysis rate constants showing that "HCN survives longest at low temperatures and neutral pH" (acid-catalyzed Ea = 67.1 kJ/mol; base-catalyzed Ea = 89.5 kJ/mol).
- *Biochemical congruence.* The route builds the pyrrolidine ring from C3 + C1 + C1 fragments (acrylonitrile + 2 HCN). Extant biology builds it from the C5 glutamate skeleton.\[10\]\[11\] The two routes are unrelated, so the route says nothing about how the biosynthetic pathway arose.

### 3. Spark discharge and archived Miller samples (Route B)

- **Ring, Wolman, Friedmann & Miller (1972, *PNAS* 69:765–768)**, "Prebiotic synthesis of hydrophobic and protein amino acids." Spark discharge on CH4/N2/H2O/NH3 gave about 25 amino acids in small amounts.\[12\] Later authors cite this work as the first identification of proline (and aspartic acid) in discharge mixtures.\[13\] Wolman, Haverland & Miller (1972, *PNAS* 69:809) compared discharge amino acids with Murchison and found similar relative distributions.\[14\] Ring & Miller (1984, *OLEB* 15:7–15) extended the work to higher hydrocarbon feedstocks.\[15\]
- **Mechanism (inferred).** Discharge chemistry makes amino acids by Strecker synthesis: HCN, aldehydes and NH3 → aminonitriles → amino acids (Miller 1957).\[16\] For proline, the analogous step would be 4-aminobutanal cyclizing to 1-pyrroline, then adding HCN to give 2-cyanopyrrolidine. No discharge study has isolated these intermediates, so this remains an inferred mechanism.
- **Archived-sample reanalyses.** Johnson et al. (2008, *Science*) reanalyzed Miller's "volcanic" apparatus extracts and found 22 amino acids and 5 amines, with chiral amino acids "racemic within the precision of the measurements."\[12\] Parker et al. (2011, *PNAS* 108:5526–5531) analyzed Miller's 1958 H2S/CH4/NH3/CO2 experiment and detected 23 amino acids and 4 amines, including 7 organosulfur compounds.\[12\]\[17\]\[18\]
- **Analytical caveat specific to proline.** Parker et al. (2011) state that "the detection of compounds containing secondary amino groups, such as sarcosine and proline, was not possible as … OPA/NAC … only derivatizes primary amino groups."\[14\] Proline's absence from modern OPA-based reanalyses is therefore a blind spot, not a negative result. Detection needs methods such as AQC or MTBSTFA derivatization, or GC-MS.
- **Critiques.** These results depend on a strongly reducing atmosphere. Cleaves et al. (2008) showed that yields in neutral CO2/N2 atmospheres can be higher than once thought, provided oxidation inhibitors are present.\[16\] Products are complex mixtures, and many experiments hydrolyze before analysis, which merges free proline with its precursors.

### 4. Extraterrestrial synthesis and delivery (Routes C–E)

**Interstellar ice analogs.**
- Muñoz Caro et al. (2002, *Nature* 416:403–406) irradiated H2O:CH3OH:NH3:CO:CO2 (2:1:1:1:1) ice at 12 K. After extraction, **acid hydrolysis**, and derivatization they identified 16 amino acids, including 6 protein amino acids (Gly, Ala, Val, Pro, Ser, Asp).\[19\]\[20\]\[21\]
- Bernstein et al. (2002, *Nature* 416:401–403) reported racemic amino acids from similar photolysis. Meinert et al. (2012) later raised the count to 20 amino acids plus 6 diamino acids with GC×GC-TOFMS.\[20\]\[22\]
- *Caveat:* proline is released from macromolecular or precursor residues only after hydrolysis. These experiments show that proline-yielding material forms, not that free proline forms.\[23\]

**Carbonaceous chondrites.**
- Proline was among the first five protein amino acids reported in Murchison (glycine, alanine, valine, proline, glutamic acid; Kvenvolden et al. 1970, *Nature*). Isotopic enrichment (13C, D) in Murchison amino acids supports an indigenous origin for much of the inventory.\[24\]

**Asteroid sample return.**
- *Bennu.* Glavin et al. (2025, *Nature Astronomy* 9:199–210) found 33 amino acids, including 14 of the 20 protein amino acids. Total amino acids were ~70 nmol g⁻¹, about 3.5× lower than Murchison, and chiral amino acids were racemic. An earlier LPSC 2024 abstract by Glavin et al. reported that "proline and asparagine were also detected in Bennu and Murchison at comparable levels using py-GC-MS." A follow-up study by Mojarro et al. (2025, *PNAS* 122:e2512461122) found that proline (as its 2tBDMS derivative) was detected in the aggregate sample (OREX-800107-0) but **not** in any of three individual stones (angular, hummocky, mottled), which contained eleven, eight and four proteinogenic amino acids respectively. The authors link reduced amino acid diversity to more intense aqueous alteration. Proline in Bennu is therefore real but spatially heterogeneous.
- *Ryugu.* Naraoka et al. (2023, *Science* 379:eabn9033) and Parker et al. (2023, *Geochim. Cosmochim. Acta* 347:42–57) reported 15 and 23 amino acids respectively, at generally <1 nmol g⁻¹ and racemic.\[25\]\[26\] Proline is not among the species these papers highlight.

**Nebular/planetesimal FTT.**
- Catalytic CO + H2 + NH3 reactions over meteoritic minerals produce amino acids (Hayatsu, Studier & Anders 1971; Yoshino et al. 1971; Pizzarello 2012, *Meteoritics & Planetary Science*), at ~360–400 K and ~10⁻⁵ atm in the nebular version.\[27\] Proline-specific products and mechanisms have not been characterized, so FTT should be treated as unproven for proline.

**Critiques.** Delivery fluxes and survival on impact are uncertain. Abundances are low (nmol g⁻¹). Detection usually combines free and hydrolyzable fractions. For chirality, see Section 7.

### 5. Hydrothermal, metal-catalyzed and protometabolic routes (Routes F–G)

**What has been demonstrated (precursor steps only).**
- **Huber & Wächtershäuser (2003, *Tetrahedron Letters*)**: FeS/Ni(OH)2-mediated reductive amination of α-keto acids to amino acids.\[28\]
- **Barge et al. (2019, *PNAS*; 2020)**: iron oxyhydroxide (green rust) drives pyruvate → alanine and related reductive aminations.
- **Muchowska, Varma & Moran (2019, *Nature* 569:104–107)**: Fe0 promotes reductive TCA-like steps in strongly acidic media (1 M HCl or H2SO4).\[29\] Making amino acids from α-keto acids under those conditions needed hydroxylamine or hydrazine.
- **Kitadai et al. (2019, *Science Advances* 5:eaav7848)**: metal/FeS precipitates under alkaline-vent-like conditions (pH ~9.6) reductively aminated four keto acids (1.25 mM each) "with the yields higher than 50%", and "alanine and glutamate were formed preferentially."\[29\]
- **Kaur et al. (2024, *Chem* 10:1528–1540)**: H2 (1–5 bar) with catalytic Ni or ground meteorites, at 22 °C and pH 8, reductively aminates six biological keto acids plus glyoxylate in "good yields" with NH4⁺ as low as 6 mM. It runs alongside reductive aldol keto-acid generation.\[30\]\[31\]
- **Mayer & Moran (2022, *Angew. Chem.*)**: using BH3CN⁻ as a model hydride, they quantified intrinsic reactivities and found α-ketoglutarate the *least* reactive biological keto acid toward reductive amination. They offer this as an explanation for why biology aminates α-ketoglutarate enzymatically.\[32\]
- González-Martínez et al. (2026, *Nature Communications* 17:6115) showed that a prebiotically plausible pyridinium/1,4-dihydropyridine pair mediates reductive amination without enzymes, reportedly giving glutamate from α-ketoglutarate in 6.5% yield. No proline formation was reported.

**What is missing for proline.** Extant biosynthesis runs:
- Glutamate → (ATP, γ-glutamyl kinase) γ-glutamyl phosphate → (NAD(P)H) glutamate-5-semialdehyde (GSA).\[11\]\[33\]\[34\]
- GSA cyclizes spontaneously and nonenzymatically to Δ1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate (P5C). Smith & Greenberg (1956, *Nature*) note that GSA "spontaneously cyclizes nearly completely."\[35\]
- P5C → (P5C reductase, NAD(P)H) L-proline.\[36\]\[37\]
- The alternative ornithine branch runs through ornithine aminotransferase (to GSA/P5C) or ornithine cyclodeaminase (to Δ1-pyrroline-2-carboxylate → Pro) (Fichman et al. 2015, *Biological Reviews*).\[10\]\[34\]\[38\]

Two steps in this chain have no prebiotic analog:
1. **Carboxylate → aldehyde reduction (Glu → GSA).** This is thermodynamically uphill without phosphoryl or thioester activation. Biology spends ATP on it, and no mineral or metal system has been shown to do it on glutamate.
2. **Imine reduction (P5C → Pro).** This is chemically easy; NaBH4 reduces P5C to proline in analytical protocols.\[39\]\[40\] But no published H2/Ni, Fe0, FeS, or photochemical prebiotic reduction of P5C or P2C to proline was found in this review.

In a dedicated search, the subagent found no Moran-group or Krishnamurthy/Springsteen-group protometabolic network (2019–2026) that reports proline. Those networks make Gly, Ala, Asp, Glu and related amino acids.\[41\] The ornithine route has only enzymatic precedents; for example, *Methanocaldococcus jannaschii* cell extracts converted 26% of ornithine to proline (*J. Bacteriol.* 2001).\[42\]\[43\]

**Hydrothermal ornithine/arginine → proline.** Vallentyne (1968, *Geochim. Cosmochim. Acta*) identified ornithine, proline and an unidentified "post-arginine" as products of aqueous arginine pyrolysis.\[44\] Körner et al. (2021, *ChemSusChem*) note that this would require the α-amino group to attack the δ-carbon, releasing NH3 or guanidine, and qualify the proline formation with "if it is true."\[45\] This is the only nonenzymatic Orn/Arg → Pro observation found. It deserves targeted re-examination with modern analytics.

**Critiques of the hydrothermal framework (for proline).**
- No demonstrated proline step.
- Keto-acid and NH3 concentrations in open vent systems are low.
- Amino acids decompose at high temperature, although proline is relatively robust (Section 6).\[46\]
- There is no plausible prebiotic carboxylate-activation chemistry for glutamate.

Supporters argue that thermodynamics favors amino acid synthesis in reduced hydrothermal fluids (next section) and that metal catalysts recapitulate the logic of core metabolism.

### 6. Thermodynamic and kinetic data

**Formation energetics.**
- Amend & Shock (1998, *Science* 281:1659–1662) found that autotrophic synthesis of all 20 protein amino acids from CO2, NH4⁺, H2 (and H2S) is energetically favored in hot (100 °C, 250 bar), moderately reduced hydrothermal fluid relative to cold, oxidized seawater. Eleven amino acids were exergonic in the hydrothermal fluid; all were endergonic in surface seawater.\[16\]\[47\]
- Kitadai (2015/2016, *OLEB*, "Energetics of amino acid synthesis in alkaline hydrothermal environments") extended revised-HKF calculations to anionic species at pH > 9.\[48\]
- Higgs & Pudritz (2009, *Astrobiology*) showed that the amino acids found in discharge experiments and meteorites correlate with lower formation free energy under surface conditions.\[16\] Proline is among the ~10 "early" amino acids they propose for the first genetic code.
- *Caveat:* single-step Gibbs energies do not capture the kinetic barriers of individual steps, especially Glu → GSA.\[49\]

**Stability of proline.**
- Vallentyne (1968, *GCA*) measured aqueous pyrolysis (0.01 M) and gave the Arrhenius expression k = 1.5 × 10¹² exp(−42,400/RT) (Ea ≈ 42.4 kcal mol⁻¹). He found proline more stable than leucine, arginine and lysine.\[44\]\[50\]
- *Our extrapolation, assuming k is in s⁻¹ (units should be checked against the original):* t½ ≈ 3 days at 250 °C, ≈ 10⁵ years at 100 °C, and ≈ 10¹¹ years at 25 °C. Proline is therefore kinetically persistent at surface and moderate-vent temperatures, but would be lost within days in >250 °C black-smoker fluids.
- Hydrothermal treatment of pure proline produces little CO2 or NH3. The main decomposition product identified is pyrrolidine from decarboxylation (Körner et al. 2021).\[45\]
- In Li & Brill's (2003, *J. Phys. Chem. A*) ranking of hydrothermal decarboxylation rates, proline decarboxylates faster than glycine, sarcosine, threonine and alanine.\[51\]
- **Intermediates.** GSA ⇌ P5C sits far toward the cyclic form, and P5C is susceptible to oxidation and hydrolysis.\[35\] α-Aminonitriles hydrolyze slowly via amides. The proline aminonitrile resists retro-Strecker reversion (Ritson 2021).\[5\]

### 7. Chirality: from racemic proline to L-proline

**Seed asymmetry.**
- *Meteorites.* Engel & Nagy (1982), Engel et al. (1990) and Engel & Macko (1997, *Nature*) reported L-excesses in Murchison protein amino acids, including about 33% L-alanine, and cited δ13C/δ15N arguments against contamination. Pizzarello & Cronin (1998, *Nature*) found interior Murchison alanine racemic. They attributed the excess to "incomplete chromatographic resolution" (co-elution) and to exterior fragments.\[52\]\[53\] Isotopic arguments have also been used to support indigenous excesses in the α-methyl amino acids (e.g., isovaline 18.5 ± 2.6% L in Murchison; Glavin & Dworkin 2009, *PNAS*), which cannot come from biological contamination.\[54\]\[55\] Claims for α-H protein amino acids such as proline remain contested because terrestrial L-contamination is most likely precisely for those compounds.\[56\]
- *Returned samples.* Pristine Ryugu and Bennu samples are racemic, which argues against a delivered L-bias.\[26\]\[57\] Bennu's contamination-knowledge program (arXiv 2026) attributes a small apparent L-valine excess possibly to laboratory background.\[58\]
- *Circularly polarized light (CPL).* UV-CPL irradiation of ice analogs induced small L-excesses (alanine up to ~1.3%, plus four other amino acids; de Marcellus et al. 2011; Modica et al. 2014, *ApJ* 788:79). Modica et al. note that proline has an anisotropy spectrum similar in sign and magnitude to alanine at 6.19 eV, so a CPL bias in proline is predicted but not measured.\[59\] CPL effects are typically ≤ a few % ee, and partial photoresolution leaves near-racemic products.\[60\]

**Amplification (proline is the best-studied case).**
- **Ternary phase behavior.** Klussmann et al. (2006, *Nature* 441:621–623) and Klussmann, White, Armstrong & Blackmond (2006, *Angew. Chem.*) showed that in a solid–solution equilibrium, solution ee is pinned at the eutectic composition. For proline the eutectic ee is 58% in methanol and 54% in ethanol at 25 °C. In CHCl3, a highly insoluble DL-proline–CHCl3 cosolvate (α = 0.002) shifts the solution eutectic to ~99% ee, so a 1% ee starting mixture can give a nearly enantiopure solution.\[61\]\[62\]\[63\] *Critiques:* chloroform is not prebiotic; "amplification" applies to the solution phase only, with the racemate locked in the solid; and the method requires a seed ee.
- **Kinetic/autocatalytic.** Mathew et al. (2004, *Angew. Chem.*) found asymmetric amplification in proline-mediated α-aminoxylation, rationalized as a kinetic resolution through a proline–product adduct.\[64\]
- **Chirality transfer to sugars.** L-proline catalyzes glyceraldehyde formation with 42% ee, favoring the unnatural L-glyceraldehyde (Breslow & Cheng 2010, *PNAS*). Hein & Blackmond (2012) showed that L-prolinate salts favor natural D-glyceraldehyde (13% ee). Wagner et al. (2017, *ACS Central Science*) showed the reverse transfer: chiral sugars enantioenrich Strecker amino acid synthesis.\[65\]\[66\]
- **Sequential amplification.** Sharma (2023, *OLEB* 53:175–185) proposes passing through conglomerate and then racemic-compound regimes to amplify from near-zero ee, modeled on aspartic acid and reaching ~50% ee. It is theoretical for most amino acids.

**Bottom line on chirality.** No route gives L-proline directly. The most credible chain is a small seed (CPL, chiral surfaces, or stochastic) followed by phase-behavior amplification, and possibly coupling to sugar chemistry. For proline, every link has been shown only in isolation and partly in non-prebiotic solvents.

## Recommendations for network design

1. **Use Route A as the mechanistic backbone for any proline node,** but add explicit nodes for: Cu(I)-catalyzed HCN + C2H2 coupling; NH3 addition; UV/HS⁻ photoreduction (254 nm); the cyclic-thioamide branch (γ-butyrothiolactam); and Strecker capture to pyrrolidine-2-carbonitrile (32%, 2 steps). Mark aminonitrile hydrolysis to proline as **unquantified**. Model cyanamide as a competing sink to the arginine aminonitrile.
2. **Encode proline's aminonitrile retention.** Unlike acyclic aminonitriles, it does not revert to the cyanohydrin at low NH3. This favors proline under fluctuating NH3 or pH.
3. **Flag the metabolism-first branch as hypothetical.** Glu formation by reductive amination is demonstrated (Fe/FeS/Ni/H2; >50% at pH ~9.6, or "good yields" at pH 8). Glu → GSA and P5C → Pro have no prebiotic demonstration and should be top experimental targets: H2/Ni or FeS reduction of P5C/P2C; thioester- or phosphate-activated glutamate reduction.
4. **Re-examine Orn/Arg → Pro hydrothermal cyclization** (Vallentyne 1968) with modern LC-MS. It is the only nonenzymatic hint of the ornithine branch.
5. **Analytics.** Use secondary-amine-competent derivatization (AQC, MTBSTFA, chiral GC-MS) and report free versus hydrolyzable proline separately. Do not read OPA-based "non-detections" of proline as absences.
6. **Temperature window.** Proline is stable for ≥10⁵ years at ≤100 °C but lost within days at ≥250 °C (extrapolated), so surface or moderate hydrothermal settings should be favored for accumulation.

## Caveats

- **Contested routes:** Route A (geochemical plausibility of sulfide, Cu, acetylene and staged delivery); Route B (atmospheric redox state); Routes C–E (mechanism unknown, hydrolysis-dependent detection, contamination); Route F (no proline step demonstrated); Route G (product identity uncertain).
- I could not read the full text or Supplementary Information of Patel et al. 2015. The step sequence and the 32% yield come from the author manuscript as quoted in search excerpts, and the 1-pyrroline intermediate is inferred. Conditions such as pH and Cu loading for the proline steps should be checked against the SI.
- The Vallentyne half-lives are my extrapolations, with uncertain rate-constant units. Treat them as order-of-magnitude.
- The finding that no prebiotic P5C → Pro reduction exists comes from a targeted but finite search, and is provisional.
