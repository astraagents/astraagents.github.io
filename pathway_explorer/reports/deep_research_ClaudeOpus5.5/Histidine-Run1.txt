# Prebiotic Synthesis of Histidine: Routes, Evidence, and Open Problems

The only reported experimental prebiotic synthesis of histidine that starts from small molecules is the 1987–1990 Shen–Yang–Miller–Oró route. Erythrose and formamidine condense to imidazole-4-acetaldehyde, and a Strecker reaction with HCN and NH₃ then gives histidine in 3.5% yield relative to erythrose.\[1\]\[2\] This route has been criticised in print as prebiotically implausible.\[3\]\[4\] No other framework (spark discharge, cyanosulfidic, hydrothermal/metabolism-first, meteoritic, network-computational) has produced a robust, quantified synthesis. Histidine is the clearest "missing amino acid" in prebiotic chemistry, and any synthesis network that includes it should treat it as a weakly supported, contested node.

## TL;DR

- **One experimental route.** Shen et al. (1987, *Origins of Life* 17:295–305; 1990, *J. Mol. Evol.* 31:167–174) made imidazole-4-acetaldehyde from erythrose and formamidine (≤1.6% yield). Adding HCN and NH₃ without isolating that intermediate gave histidine at 3.5% relative to erythrose. Every other reported abiotic detection is a trace or tentative identification, mostly from HCN polymers and spark discharges.
- **The route is contested.** Vázquez-Salazar and colleagues (2016, *Orig. Life Evol. Biosph.*; 2018, *PLoS ONE*) note three problems. The route needs 0.3 M formamidine, which hydrolyses quickly. Tetroses are minor, unstable formose products. And histidine has never been found in any carbonaceous chondrite or in returned asteroid samples (Bennu: Mojarro et al. 2025, *PNAS*). Their conclusion is that histidine may be a product of early biology rather than prebiotic chemistry.
- **Recommendation for network design.** Model histidine as a late, low-flux product reached through imidazole-4-acetaldehyde plus a Strecker step. Treat the C4-sugar + amidine imidazole-forming step as the main bottleneck. Keep a parallel "biotic-origin" hypothesis (a semi-enzymatic, IGP-like route) in the network. Thermodynamic and abundance-ranking analyses (Higgs & Pudritz 2009, *Astrobiology*) place histidine outside the prebiotic "early" set.

## Key Findings

| Framework | Feedstocks | Status for histidine | Main critique |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sugar + amidine → Strecker (Shen/Oró/Miller) | H₂CO-derived erythrose, formamidine (from HCN/NH₃), HCN, NH₃ | Demonstrated, 3.5% His/erythrose | 0.3 M formamidine; unstable tetroses; not linked to extant biosynthesis |
| Erythrose + H₂CO + NH₃ (Shen et al. 1987) | Erythrose, formaldehyde, ammonia | Gives imidazole-4-ethanol (5.4%) and imidazole-4-glycol (6.8%), which would still need conversion to the aldehyde | Extra redox/oxidation steps not demonstrated in the same pot |
| HCN polymer hydrolysis | HCN/NH₄CN | Histidine reported in older acid-hydrolysate analyses | Old analytical methods; trace levels; not independently confirmed |
| Spark discharge (Miller–Urey type) | CH₄/N₂/H₂/H₂O, redox-neutral mixes | "Traces" or tentative HPLC-only assignment | Not confirmed by MS; reviewers state no imidazole amino acid produced |
| Cyanosulfidic (Sutherland) | HCN, H₂S, Cu, UV | No histidine route reported | HCN chemistry reaches many amino acids, but not histidine |
| Hydrothermal / metabolism-first | CO₂, H₂, NH₃ | Thermodynamic calculations only; no experiment | Histidine synthesis is energetically costly and has no experimental pathway |
| Exogenous delivery | Meteorites/asteroids | Not detected (Murchison, Aguas Zarcas, Bennu) | Imidazole carboxylic acids are present, but histidine is absent |
| Biotic / semi-enzymatic origin | PRPP, ATP, NH₄⁺ | Proposed (Lazcano & Miller; Vázquez-Salazar et al. 2018) | Assumes histidine was not needed before enzymes existed |

## Details

### 1. Route A — Erythrose + formamidine → imidazole-4-acetaldehyde → Strecker → histidine

**Reaction steps.**
1. Formaldehyde (via the formose reaction) gives tetroses (D-erythrose or D-threose).
2. Erythrose condenses with formamidine (HC(=NH)NH₂, an HCN/NH₃-derived amidine). The imidazole ring closes through an Amadori-type rearrangement, giving imidazole-4-acetaldehyde together with imidazole-4-glycol.\[5\]
3. HCN and NH₃ are added to the unpurified mixture. A Strecker sequence (aminonitrile, then hydrolysis) gives histidine.\[2\]

**Yields.** Shen, Yang, Miller & Oró (1987, *Origins of Life* 17:295–305) reported maximum yields, based on erythrose, of:\[6\]
- imidazole-4-acetaldehyde: 1.6%\[7\]
- imidazole-4-ethanol: 5.4% (from the erythrose + formaldehyde + ammonia variant)\[8\]
- imidazole-4-glycol: 6.8%\[7\]

In that paper the conversion to histidine was argued rather than shown. They wrote that imidazole-4-acetaldehyde "would have been converted to histidine on the primitive earth by a Strecker synthesis," and that other prebiotic reactions could convert the glycol and ethanol to the aldehyde.\[7\]

The 1990 follow-up (Shen, Yang, Miller & Oró, *J. Mol. Evol.* 31:167–174) carried out the second step. The authors converted imidazole-4-acetaldehyde to histidine "without isolation of the reaction products by adding HCN and ammonia to the reaction mixture." They identified the product by LC, HPLC, thermospray LC-MS and tandem MS, at 3.5% based on the ratio of histidine to erythrose.\[1\] A companion paper (Shen, Mills & Oró 1990, *J. Mol. Evol.* 31:175–179) made His-His in up to 14.4% yield using histidine, cyanamide and 4-amino-5-imidazole carboxamide; a trace of His trimer was also seen.\[9\]

**Conditions and catalysts.** This is aqueous, ambient-to-moderate-temperature chemistry that needs no mineral or metal catalyst. Condensation, ring closure and the Strecker step all proceed uncatalysed. Exact temperature, pH, reaction time and erythrose/HCN molarities could not be retrieved from the paywalled originals for this report. The only concentration on record is formamidine at 0.3 M, quoted by later critics.\[10\] Users building kinetic models should take the remaining parameters directly from the 1987 and 1990 papers.

**Chemical precedent.** John Oró told Lazcano that the ring closure was based on Ames's (1957, *J. Biol. Chem.*) chemical synthesis of imidazole glycerol phosphate from ribose-5-phosphate and formamidine.\[11\] So the route is borrowed from biochemical model chemistry, not found in a geochemical simulation.

**Published critiques.**
- **Formamidine.** Vázquez-Salazar, Becerra & Lazcano (2018, *PLoS ONE* 13:e0196349) note that formamidine "is a labile compound that is rapidly hydrolyzed into formic acid and ammonia." That makes the required 0.3 M concentration "very difficult to achieve."\[3\]\[12\]
- **Tetroses.** The same authors point out that D-erythrose and D-threose "are minor, unstable products of the formose reaction" and "could not have been available in the concentration required."\[4\]\[12\] They conclude that this "strongly hinders the prebiotic significance of this synthesis."\[12\]
- **Earlier critique.** Vázquez-Salazar et al. (2016, *Orig. Life Evol. Biosph.*) called the synthesis "inefficient" and said "neither the reactants nor the conditions are truly prebiotic."\[10\]\[11\]
- **Biochemical congruence.** The abiotic ring closure, like the HisF/IGP synthase reaction, goes through an Amadori rearrangement.\[4\] Even so, Vázquez-Salazar et al. argue there is "no direct evolutionary connection between the abiotic synthesis reported by Shen et al. and the extant histidine anabolism."\[12\]
- **Points in the route's favour.** Erythrose stereochemistry does not matter, because the stereocentres are lost, and any C4 aldose would work.\[4\]\[12\] The Strecker step is the same general chemistry that makes most prebiotic α-amino acids. In parent-body Strecker models (Cobb, Pudritz & Pearce 2015, *ApJ* 809:6), histidine is explicitly paired with imidazole-4-acetaldehyde as its aldehyde precursor.\[13\]

**Assessment.** The route is chemically sound but prebiotically contested. Its weak point is not the Strecker step but making and accumulating the C4 sugar and the amidine at the same time in one environment.

### 2. Route B — Forming the imidazole ring without the sugar–amidine step

Oró, Basile, Cortes, Shen & Yamrom (1984, *Origins of Life* 14:237–242) made imidazole and 2-methylimidazole from glyoxal, an aldehyde (formaldehyde or acetaldehyde) and ammonia, a Debus-type condensation.\[14\] They made 4-methylimidazole from a carbohydrate and ammonia.\[15\] These experiments show that imidazole rings form readily from H₂CO/NH₃-derived precursors without catalysts. However, they give the wrong substitution pattern: none carries the 4-CH₂-CHO side chain that histidine needs. No paper retrieved here demonstrates side-chain elongation of a simple imidazole to imidazole-4-acetaldehyde under prebiotic conditions.

The PNAS Bennu study (Mojarro, Aponte, Dworkin, Elsila, Glavin, Connolly & Lauretta 2025, *PNAS* 122:e2512461122) detected 2- and 4-imidazole carboxylic acid. It called them "directly relevant precursors or intermediates for histidine formation through reductive amination, decarboxylation, or side-chain elongation reactions."\[16\] This is speculation: the reference cited for it is a review of biological histidine biosynthesis, and no prebiotic experiment is shown.\[17\]

### 3. HCN-polymer and spark-discharge reports (trace or tentative)

- **HCN polymers.** Ruiz-Bermejo, Zorzano & Osuna-Esteban (2013, *Life* 3:421–448) compiled histidine among amino acids released by acid hydrolysis (6 N HCl, 100–110 °C, 16–24 h) of the brown precipitate from 1 M NH₄CN heated at 90 °C for 4 h.\[18\]\[19\] Older work on anhydrous HCN polymers (Matthews & Moser lineage) also listed histidine among hydrolysis products.\[20\] These reports date mostly from the 1960s–1980s and used amino-acid-analyser or chromatographic identification. They have not been confirmed by modern isotope-labelled MS.
- **Spark discharge.** Zaia, Zaia & De Santana (2008, *Orig. Life Evol. Biosph.*) list "α-histidine-traces" from Plankensteiner et al. (2006) in redox-neutral atmospheres. They also list a trace from Ruiz-Bermejo et al. (2007), a CH₄/N₂/H₂ spark with aqueous aerosol, where histidine was "tentatively assigned by HPLC only." Other 2008 work could not confirm α-histidine.\[21\]\[22\] Modern reanalyses of Miller's archived samples found 22 amino acids in the volcanic apparatus (Johnson et al. 2008, *Science*) and 23 amino acids in the 1958 H₂S experiment (Parker et al. 2011, *PNAS*), but neither list includes histidine.\[23\]\[24\]
- **Interpretation.** These are the only feedstock-to-histidine hints that skip a pre-made sugar. They point to HCN/NH₃ chemistry as a possible source of imidazole-bearing intermediates, but they are not robust enough to anchor a network edge. Vázquez-Salazar et al. (2018) state outright that "no imidazole-bearing amino acid has been produced in Miller-Urey-type experiments or in other type of laboratory simulations."\[3\]\[12\] That is stronger than the trace literature strictly supports, and the two positions should be recorded as a disagreement.

### 4. Cyanosulfidic chemistry

The cyanosulfidic network (Patel, Percivalle, Ritson, Duffy & Sutherland 2015, *Nature Chemistry* 7:301–307) uses HCN, H₂S, Cu(I)/Cu(II), phosphate and UV light. It reaches precursors of many proteinogenic amino acids, ribonucleotides and lipids, but no histidine route has been reported from it.\[25\] Reviewers have noted that HCN is a precursor to purines, pyrimidines and amino acids "but not histidine" (Vázquez-Salazar et al. 2016).\[11\] The same group has since made imidazole-based activating agents, for example N-cyanoimidazole from thiocyanogen plus imidazole (Marinic, Ritson, Wordsworth & Sutherland 2026, *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 148:37623–37627). This shows that imidazole fits within that chemistry even though the histidine side chain has not been reached. The open question for this framework is whether a C4 sugar or an equivalent C2 + C2 fragment can be coupled with an HCN-derived amidine under its UV/sulfide conditions.

### 5. Hydrothermal / metabolism-first framework

- **Thermodynamics.** Amend & Shock (1998, *Science* 281:1659–1662) wrote net histidine synthesis as 6CO₂ + 3NH₄⁺ + 10H₂ → histidine + 3H⁺ + 10H₂O. They found net synthesis exergonic for 11 of 20 amino acids in a 100 °C mixed hydrothermal fluid, versus surface seawater. In the indexed Table 3, the histidine row lists ΔG-like values of about 154 and 351 kJ/mol.\[26\] That is consistent with histidine being endergonic in both fluids, though the column headings could not be checked directly here. Either way, histidine is among the most expensive amino acids to make.
- **Abundance ranking.** Higgs & Pudritz (2009, *Astrobiology* 9:483–490) found that the 10 "early" amino acids (G, A, D, E, V, S, I, L, P, T) can be ranked by prebiotic abundance across meteorites, discharge experiments and hydrothermal simulations, and that the ranking follows free energy of formation. Histidine falls outside this set and is classed as a later addition that needed biosynthesis.\[27\]\[28\]
- **Experiments.** No hydrothermal, Fischer–Tropsch-type, or iron/nickel-mineral experiment retrieved here has made histidine or imidazole-4-pyruvate. Non-enzymatic reductive amination and transamination of α-keto acids have been quantified for pyruvate, oxaloacetate and α-ketoglutarate (e.g., Mayer et al. 2022, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.*), and Kaur et al. (2024, *Chem*) aminated glyoxylate and six α-keto acids with H₂ over Ni/Pd/meteorite catalysts with "ammonia as low as 6 mM"; an NADH-mediated reductive amination runs "at room temperature in ammonium/ammonia solutions at pH > 7" (2024, *Org. Chem. Front.* 11:1924–1932). Imidazole-4-pyruvate was not among the tested substrates. By analogy, imidazole-4-pyruvate would give histidine, but no prebiotic synthesis of imidazole-4-pyruvate exists.\[29\] For network design, metabolism-first histidine is currently a hypothesis with no experimental edges.

### 6. Exogenous delivery

- **Meteorites.** Histidine has not been found in carbonaceous chondrites.\[30\]\[31\] Glavin et al. (2021, *Meteoritics & Planetary Science*) note that protein amino acids such as "tyrosine, phenylalanine, lysine, histidine, arginine" were absent from Aguas Zarcas and Murchison, consistent with no terrestrial contamination.\[32\]
- **Bennu.** Glavin, Dworkin et al. (2025, *Nature Astronomy* 9:199–210) reported 33 amino acids, 14 of them proteinogenic, and all five nucleobases in Bennu samples.\[33\]\[34\] Mojarro et al. (2025, *PNAS*) detected 15 of the 20 protein amino acids in the aggregate sample but state: "We did not observe cysteine, glutamine, lysine, histidine, and arginine."\[16\]\[17\] The pristine, contamination-controlled sample-return data therefore extend the meteoritic absence of histidine. They do, however, establish that imidazole carboxylic acids form abiotically on carbonaceous parent bodies.\[16\]\[35\]

### 7. Biotic or semi-enzymatic origin

- **White's vestige hypothesis.** White (1976) proposed that histidine is the molecular vestige of a catalytic nucleotide from an RNA world. Histidine biosynthesis starts from PRPP + ATP and shares the AICAR intermediate with purine synthesis.\[3\]\[12\]
- **Convergent evolution.** Vázquez-Salazar et al. (2018) show that the imidazole rings of histidine and purines are made by unrelated enzymes (HisF and PurM share 12.2% sequence identity; RMSD 8.27 Å), which they read as convergent evolution.\[12\] They suggest purine biosynthesis came before histidine biosynthesis.\[3\]
- **Semi-enzymatic IGP formation.** In vitro, HisF alone forms IGP at high NH₄⁺, and *Klebsiella pneumoniae* hisH mutants recover function at high NH₄⁺.\[5\] Lazcano & Miller argued that this marks a "semi-enzymatic" stage, in which early enzymes worked together with a favourable chemical environment.\[3\]
- **Phylogenetic counterpoint.** LUCA protein-domain analysis (Wehbi et al. 2024, *PNAS*) finds histidine enriched in the oldest domains, including the HIGH motif of class I aaRSs. They argue that "the lack of abiotic availability was key to H's previous annotation as late, but biotic availability of H in an RNA-dominant biotic context would have been sufficient."\[36\]
- **Synthesis.** Phylogeny says histidine was used very early; abiotic chemistry says it was scarce. A biotic or semi-biotic supply reconciles the two.

### 8. Catalysts and surfaces

- **Catalysts for histidine formation.** None have been shown. Every demonstrated step (imidazole condensation, Amadori ring closure, Strecker) is uncatalysed.
- **Mineral roles after formation.** Bernal, Mejía Luna et al. (2021, *Orig. Life Evol. Biosph.* 51:117–130) found that histidine self-assembles and is stabilised on antigorite and aragonite surfaces; pyrite was also tested.
- **Histidine as a catalyst.** Transition metals enhance depsipeptide oligomerization involving histidine (Frenkel-Pinter et al. 2021, *RSC Adv.*).\[37\] Maguire, Smokers, Oosterom, Zheliezniak & Huck (2024, *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 146:7839–7849) showed that histidyl peptides catalyse phosphorylation by imidazole phosphate formed from orthophosphate, cyanate and imidazole, "via a phosphorylated histidyl intermediate"; a *Nature Catalysis* highlight reported a "two-orders-of-magnitude acceleration compared to the uncatalysed reaction." As summarised by Maguire et al., earlier work showed His-His enhancing dephosphorylation of dAMP, hydrolysis of oligo(A)₁₂ and 2′,3′-cAMP oligomerization under wet–dry cycling.
- **Implication.** Histidine is better supported as a prebiotic catalyst than as a prebiotic product.

### 9. Computational proposals

- **Allchemy.** The Allchemy forward-synthesis network (Wołos et al. 2020, *Science* 369:eaaw1955) cites the Shen et al. histidine route.\[38\] No reported histidine-specific experimental validation from that work was found.
- **Mg-porphin route.** A quantum-chemical proposal (Aylward 2012, WSEAS) for photochemical histidine formation from HCN, diacetylene, NH₃, CO and H₂ on a Mg-porphin catalyst is purely computational and appears in a low-visibility venue.\[39\] It should not be treated as evidence.

## Environmental Context

No environment is well supported for histidine specifically. Ranked by evidence:

1. **Surface or near-surface aqueous pools with formose plus HCN/NH₃ chemistry** (evaporitic or wet–dry settings). This is the only setting with a demonstrated route (Shen et al.). It needs simultaneous tetrose and amidine accumulation and gives a few per cent yield. Evaporative concentration could help reach 0.3 M-scale amidine, but formamidine hydrolysis works against accumulation. This trade-off has not been tested experimentally.
2. **Reducing-atmosphere discharge or HCN-rich settings.** Only trace or tentative detections exist.
3. **Hydrothermal systems.** Calculations only, and histidine is among the least favourable amino acids.
4. **Exogenous delivery.** Ruled out as a significant source on current evidence (chondrites, Bennu), although imidazole precursors are delivered.

## Open Questions and Bottlenecks

1. **Imidazole ring with the correct C4 side chain.** This is the central bottleneck. Simple Debus-type imidazoles form easily, but a regioselective 4-substituted imidazole with a two-carbon aldehyde arm has been shown only from a pre-formed C4 sugar.
2. **Supply of tetroses and formamidine together.** Tetroses are minor, unstable formose products. Formamidine hydrolyses quickly. Neither concentration has been modelled against realistic fluxes.
3. **Independent replication.** The Shen et al. histidine result (1990) has no published independent reproduction or modern isotope-labelled confirmation in the sources retrieved.
4. **Compatibility with other steps.** The Strecker step needs HCN/NH₃. Imidazole-4-acetaldehyde also competes with cyanohydrin (giving the α-hydroxy acid) and with oxidation or reduction to the glycol or ethanol. No study reports selectivity of histidine over the hydroxy-acid by-product.
5. **Hydrothermal pathway.** Imidazole-4-pyruvate has not been made abiotically, so reductive amination or transamination routes have nothing to start from.
6. **Absence in extraterrestrial material.** Is the absence real, or a matter of detection limits or degradation (for example histidine's oxidative lability)? Current data cannot tell.
7. **Fit with extant biochemistry.** Abiotic routes share the Amadori ring-closure chemistry with biosynthesis but not its intermediates (PRPP, PRFAR, IGP). Whether early life inherited histidine or invented it remains open.

## Recommendations for Synthesis-Network Design

- **Represent histidine through one validated edge set.** Use H₂CO → (formose) → erythrose; HCN + NH₃ → formamidine; erythrose + formamidine → imidazole-4-acetaldehyde (≤1.6%); imidazole-4-acetaldehyde + HCN + NH₃ → histidine (3.5% overall relative to erythrose). Flag every edge as "contested: concentration-limited."
- **Add side branches.** Include the imidazole-4-glycol (6.8%) and imidazole-4-ethanol (5.4%) branches as sinks or reservoirs, and mark their conversion to the aldehyde as undemonstrated.
- **Weight the edges low.** Do not assign hydrothermal, cyanosulfidic or meteoritic edges to histidine. If a network uses abundance-weighted amino-acid inputs, histidine should be low or zero, consistent with Higgs & Pudritz (2009) and the Bennu/chondrite data.
- **Add a "biotic histidine" alternative.** Use a semi-enzymatic, IGP-like route needing high NH₄⁺, so it can be compared with the abiotic route.
- **Highest-value experiments to fill gaps:**
  - a modern, isotope-labelled reproduction of the Shen et al. route with full conditions
  - tetrose plus amidine accumulation under wet–dry cycling
  - abiotic imidazole-4-pyruvate synthesis
  - side-chain elongation of imidazole-4-carboxylic acid or aldehyde

## Caveats

- The exact temperature, pH, reaction time and reactant molarities of the Shen et al. experiments could not be retrieved; only formamidine at 0.3 M is documented through secondary sources.
- The HCN-polymer and spark-discharge histidine detections come from review tables. The primary 1960s–2000s reports were not inspected.
- The Amend & Shock histidine ΔG values are read from an indexed table without verified column headings.
- Absence of evidence in some frameworks (cyanosulfidic, hydrothermal) reflects what was searched, not proof that no study exists.
